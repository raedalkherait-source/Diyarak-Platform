# ADR-0061: Search-engine visibility and optimized listing images

## Status

Accepted

## Context

Most marketplace traffic comes from search engines. Listing pages already rendered on the server,
but crawlers had no sitemap, no structured data and no canonical URLs. Listing cards and the
gallery also downloaded the original uploaded photo, which can be close to a megabyte. That is
slow and costly on mobile connections in Syria.

## Decision

Search-engine visibility (portal):

- `/robots.txt` allows public pages, disallows `/my`, `/admin`, `/login` and `/api/`, and points to
  the sitemap. It is rendered per request so it follows `PORTAL_PUBLIC_URL` at runtime.
- `/sitemap.xml` lists the home page, the search page, a Sell and Rent landing page per
  governorate, and published listings up to the public API's 10,000-row window. It is
  regenerated hourly. If the API is unreachable it still returns the static entries.
- Listing pages embed schema.org `RealEstateListing` JSON-LD (name, URL, images, an address in
  Syria, and an `Offer` when a price is known), escaped for safe embedding, plus a canonical URL.
- Search pages are indexed only when they use governorate, offer type and category filters on the
  first page. Deeper filter combinations are `noindex, follow`.

Optimized images (portal):

- Listing cards and the public gallery use `next/image`, restricted by `localPatterns` to
  `/media/listings/**`. External URLs and private paths are rejected with `400`.
- Variants are produced in AVIF or WebP at sizes up to 1600 px and cached for a year, since
  photo URLs are immutable. Draft photos in the dashboard keep using the private, uncached route.
- In a measurement on a 2000×1333 photo of 945 KB, a 640-px variant came to 16 KB as WebP and
  5 KB as AVIF. The search page on a phone loaded 17 KB of images for 8 listings.
- Image optimization runs inside the portal container (sharp for musl is included in the
  standalone build).

## Consequences

Crawlers can discover and understand every published listing, and pages load much faster on slow
connections. Optimized variants are cached in the portal container and regenerated after a
restart. A persistent cache volume, or a CDN in front of the portal, avoids that work at scale.
