# ADR-0043: No-store cache policy for authenticated Market management endpoints

## Status

Accepted and implemented.

## Context

The authenticated Market management APIs now expose complete owner-scoped Property state and Listing management state. Property responses include address and other asset details, while Listing responses include management-only status and version information. These routes are protected by the ADR-0025 mapped-user policy, but authentication alone does not define how user agents or intermediary caches should retain successful or error responses.

The anonymous public Listing routes introduced by ADR-0038 and ADR-0039 intentionally have a different exposure boundary and must not inherit a management-only cache policy by path accident or by a global response rule.

## Decision

Every mapped authenticated Market management route is marked with dedicated endpoint metadata at mapping time.

A Host middleware runs after routing and before authentication/authorization. When the selected endpoint has that management metadata, the middleware registers a final response callback that sets:

`Cache-Control: no-store`

This applies to the complete response produced for the selected management endpoint, including successful responses and expected validation, authentication, authorization, not-found, and conflict outcomes. It therefore also covers `401` and `403` responses that may be produced before the route handler executes.

The route-mapping helper combines the existing ADR-0025 mapped-user authorization requirement with the management cache metadata so newly mapped Market management routes must opt into both concerns together.

Anonymous `/api/market/public/...` endpoints do not receive the management metadata and are not assigned `no-store` by this policy. This ADR does not define a positive public caching lifetime, validators, CDN behavior, or any other cache semantics for public responses.

## Consequences

Authenticated Market management responses are explicitly unsuitable for storage by browsers, shared proxies, or other HTTP caches that honor `Cache-Control`. The policy is centralized instead of being repeated in every route handler and applies even when authorization short-circuits execution.

No JSON representation, status code, authentication decision, ownership rule, pagination contract, persistence schema, or public Listing behavior changes. Public cacheability remains a separate future transport decision.
